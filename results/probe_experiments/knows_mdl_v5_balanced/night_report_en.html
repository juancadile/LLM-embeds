Thirteen Kilobytes of Knowledge

How large is the rule by which Qwen3-14B decides whether someone knows something. We attempted four measures on its frozen representations; three produced usable estimates.

For the paper: “A 13 kB bound on Qwen3-14B's KNOWS rule”.

BOTTOM LINE UP FRONT

What we did and what it shows

  • •Connection to Jens's paper.

    We empirically tested its idea that a classifier provides a constructive upper bound on the size of a concept's decision rule.

  • •What we trained.

    Small probes on Qwen3-14B's extracted internal activations, using Qwen's own yes/no judgments as target labels — not its answer text as input.

  • •Separation of inputs and labels.

    Activations came from a separate scenario-only pass, with no predicate question, answer choices, or generated answer present. Qwen remained frozen.

  • •Main result.

    A linear probe reproduced Qwen's KNOWS judgments with 0.980 macro-F1, using a compressed payload of 13,210 bytes (median of five seeds).

  • •What that bound means.

    It is conditional on Qwen's pretrained representations being available. It bounds an approximate decoder of Qwen's judgments, not the absolute complexity of human knowledge.

  • •Other measurements.

    Intrinsic-dimensionality descriptions did not beat direct compression once preprocessing was counted. Online MDL measures label extractability and learning efficiency — a complementary quantity, not classifier size.

  • •Bottom line.

    This is a concrete implementation and stress test of Jens's classifier-bound proposal, with explicit limits on what the resulting number can claim.

01

The question, and why this way of answering it

The paper wants to put a number on the complexity of a concept. The reviewer's objection had two parts, and both point to the same place.

First: the 100 kB chain assumes parameters are incompressible. They are not — quantization and pruning are standard and lose almost no accuracy, so the naive bound is loose “by an order of magnitude” before the intrinsic-dimension move even enters.

Second, and more fundamentally: if you prompt an LLM as a classifier, the classifier is the whole model, and the upper bound on JUSTICE ends up being the size of GPT‑4. That tells you nothing. The interesting version is something bounded — a small probe trained on the model's representations — where the size actually means something.

This report measures exactly that, across five epistemic predicates, with the model's own answers as ground truth. We are not measuring the human concept of knowledge; we are measuring how much it costs to extract, from the model's activations, its own decision rule.

02

How it is measured

Five steps, all with controls.

  1. iScenario bank.

    2,048 epistemic situations in a factorial design — someone believes something, has such-and-such evidence, it turns out true or not, they got lucky or not — each rewritten in several paraphrases: 6,144 texts. Paraphrases of the same scenario never cross splits.

  2. iiThe model's own labels.

    Five yes/no questions per scenario: does she know? believe? is it true? is she justified? was it a lucky guess? Only answers stable under option order are retained.

  3. iiiFrozen activations.

    All 40 layers plus the embedding are read, with model and tokenizer revisions pinned and no truncation.

  4. ivTiny probes.

    Linear and 256-unit MLP, trained to predict the model's answers. If a small probe reproduces them, the rule is simple relative to that representation.

  5. vFour size measures, three usable.

    Online codelength in the style of Voita–Titov (how many bits it costs to transmit the labels while learning as you go), intrinsic dimension by random subspace, and serialized bytes of the compressed probe. The fourth, the local learning coefficient, turned out not to be identifiable. Controls throughout: shuffled labels, shuffled representations, random projections.

The four hypotheses of this batch were written with numerical predictions and falsification criteria before anything was run, in NIGHT_EXPERIMENTS.md. There was no external time-stamped registry, so the exact wording is “prospectively prespecified predictions”, not “preregistration”. The predictions were left unedited; results and corrections were recorded beneath them.

03

The bound: from 5.2 MB to 13 kB

397× smaller than the naive description, retaining 0.980 macro-F1 on data never used to select anything.

The starting point was a finding already in the data that had gone unnoticed: the linear probe is as cheap as or cheaper than the MLP for all five predicates. The rule is linearly decodable, so quoting the bound on a 1,311,233-parameter MLP overstates it.

An honest description has to be applicable to the raw activations. A probe trained on standardized inputs becomes one on raw inputs by folding mean and scale into its first affine layer — w·((x−m)/s)+b = (w/s)·x + b', exact, verified at a maximum relative logit difference of 5.7·10⁻⁷. Only after folding is it compressed, and compression is judged on raw activations.

Where the 397× comes from. Almost all of it comes from discovering that a linear probe suffices, not from quantization: going from the float32 MLP to the float32 linear probe gives 256× (5,244,932 → 20,484); quantizing the linear probe gives a further 1.55× (20,484 → 13,210). Compressing the MLP without changing architecture gives only 29.5×. This does not weaken the result; it makes it more precise.

Why 13 kB and not less

The folded weights w/s have a much wider dynamic range than the standardized ones, so coarse quantization fails the declared acceptance rule (≤ 0.05 nats of loss and ≤ 1 accuracy point on tuning). At 4 bits the probe collapses to the majority class (macro-F1 0.22). At 8 bits it sits right on the boundary: it passes for two seeds out of five, and for those two the payload drops to 8,089 bytes. At 16 bits it always passes. The median across the five seeds is 13,210 bytes; for the MLP, two seeds need rank 16 and rise to 349,570.

The intrinsic-dimension move does not tighten the bound

This is the uncomfortable result, and the one that would interest the reviewer most. The subspace θ₀ + P·φ reconstructs a probe over standardized inputs and, unlike a direct probe, cannot absorb the standardizer: applying it requires the 40,960 bytes of mean and scale. With those, the linear subspace costs 43,016 bytes — more than twice the compressed linear probe, and more even than the uncompressed float32 linear probe (20,484). And the standardizer is fit per predicate on that predicate's own stable cohort, so it cannot be called shared overhead either: a design with a single per-layer standardizer would be a different experiment, which was not run.

E1 fails on its own criterion

The prespecified predictions said the experiment failed “if the subspace description does not beat direct compression”. It does not. The 397× reduction survives as a result; the experiment as declared does not, and it stands as failed/mixed in the ledger. Two sub-predictions also failed: the compressed linear probe did not get below 10,000 B (13,210), and the linear probe's d90 was not “well below” 1,024 (512, a factor of two).

04

The composition: knowledge from two numbers

If the model's KNOWS were approximately a function of its own belief, justification and truth rules, then its knowledge labels should be cheap to code using only the outputs of those three probes.

They are. Three numbers cost 0.139 bits per label against 0.129 from the full 5,120 dimensions: a ratio of 1.08, well inside the 1.5 ceiling I had declared. A nonlinear summary based on the JTB components preserves almost all of the codelength of the full representation.

The features are out-of-fold logits from 5-fold cross-fitting over independent scenario groups: no example is scored by a probe that saw it, or saw any paraphrase of it.

  • →The composition is not additive.

    A linear probe reads KNOWS off the raw activations more cheaply than an MLP (0.119 versus 0.129). But on the three component signals, the linear readout costs 3.18× and the nonlinear one 1.08×. This is compatible with a conjunctive composition; it does not show that conjunction is the model's internal operation.

  • →Belief adds no detectable improvement — and its necessity is not identifiable.

    Justification + truth alone reach 0.139, tied with all three; belief alone (0.596) improves only marginally on a random three-dimensional projection (0.618), with no evidence of separation. But the cohort cannot separate the two readings: of the 1,701 examples with JUSTIFIED=1 and TRUE=1, exactly one has BELIEVES=0. With that collinearity there is no support on which an incremental effect of belief could show.

  • →The controls behave.

    Shuffled components land at 0.852, close to the marginal of 0.902 and far from the real components (0.139).

Gettier: the prediction was not falsifiable, and two variables must be kept apart

I predicted that residual cost would concentrate on the cases the model judges a lucky guess. The comparison turned out to be impossible: in the evaluation split, all 344 cases with lucky_guessed = 1 have knows = 0 without exception — marginal entropy exactly 0.0000 bits, against 0.9804 for the rest. A constant subset is cheaper than one with almost a bit of entropy whatever the probe does.

Moreover, two different things had been run together. lucky_guessed = 1 is a judgement the model produced (1,563 examples in the cohort); gettier = True is a factor of the scenario design (418). On the 418 design-Gettier cases, the model grants knowledge in zero and calls 395 a lucky guess. On its own luck judgement, it grants knowledge in 2 of 1,563. Both show near-total exclusion of KNOWS, but they are not the same variable, and those cases show no higher decoding cost under this protocol; there is no evidence here of the human philosophical difficulty with them.

05

Depth: two thirds of the way, and not the end

Every predicate reaches its minimum between layers 26 and 29 of 40 — between 65% and 72% of depth — under both probe kinds, ten cells out of ten. I had declared the 24–36 window and they fell in a three-layer band.

Five different epistemic predicates agreeing like this suggests that the depth is a property of the model's processing, not of the concept being decoded. The final layer is generally worse.

06

The ordering between concepts: two claims survive

The descriptive table suggested a complexity ordering among the five predicates. A paired bootstrap of 400 resamples of the scenario bank, with five probe seeds averaged per draw, decided which differences hold up under this procedure. The scope is limited: E3 compares normalized MDL with a linear probe at layer 29 on the common cohort; it does not measure absolute complexity or each predicate's own minimum.

The intervals from the first run were withdrawn

External review found that the bootstrap resampled scenario groups with replacement but kept each copy's original identifier. The online coder then merged repeated copies as if they were a single group: on the coding split, of 720 sampled groups only about 455 distinct identifiers (63%) reached the coder. That changes the number of groups, the shuffle order and every prequential endpoint.

All summaries produced by the defective bootstrap are withdrawn — medians, intervals and probabilities — because the defect altered the effective size, order and endpoints of every draw. The unresampled estimates remain as descriptive results. The defect is fixed at the root shared by the bootstrap and the joint-refit driver — every sampled copy now enters as its own group, with a regression test that checks it in every split.

A second review found that the corrected run was training the probe with a single seed (2026) per draw, although the protocol declares five. That run was kept as a control (its levels match the confirmatory run within 0.005; it only had wider intervals), and the confirmatory analysis was done to protocol: 400 draws × five seeds, averaged per draw before building the ranking. What follows is that result.

Confirmatory result: corrected bootstrap, five seeds

400 clean draws, each copy as its own group, five probe seeds averaged per draw before ranking. Zero degenerate resamples. Averaging the five fixed seeds estimates mean performance over them and reduces training variability; it does not incorporate all of it. The intervals are scenario-resampling intervals for that average, not a joint bootstrap over scenarios and seeds.

Cheapest: justified 61.8%, believes 31.0%, true 7.2%, knows 0%. Dearest: lucky_guessed 100%. Three verdicts: the prediction about believes fails (31.0% against the declared 80%); the one about lucky_guessed holds; and the non-separation prediction for justification, knowledge and truth fails in part: justified is separated from knows for the seed average (−0.0168, 95% CI [−0.0288, −0.0065]; also at 99.5% [−0.0367, −0.0033]). The direction is the same in all five seeds (medians between −0.015 and −0.017, justified cheaper in 95–98% of draws), but the individual interval contains zero for three of them and touches zero for the other two. The separation is clear for the average, not for any single training run. The difference is small — 15% of the codelength, against the ~60% gap to lucky_guessed.

What the study can claim about ordering, with intervals excluding zero under paired scenario resampling and five seeds: the lucky-guess rule is the most expensive to decode, and the justification rule is cheaper to decode than the knowledge rule. Belief, truth, knowledge and justification are not ranked among themselves beyond that; in particular, knowledge does not separate from belief or from truth — which does not show that they are equally complex, only that this measurement does not distinguish them.

07

Negative results, plainly

  • ×Replication failed in both secondary models.

    Neither Qwen3-1.7B nor Llama-3.1-8B passes the prespecified threshold of 1,000 stable examples per class, for any of the five predicates. Llama produced 4,257 positives and zero stable negatives for KNOWS. With this prompt they say yes to almost everything, so the study speaks about Qwen3-14B and not about “language models”. The failure is informative about the labeling method, not about the complexity measure.

  • ×The local learning coefficient is not identifiable here.

    All twelve declared candidates were rejected. The standard mixing diagnostics are nominal (R-hat 0.999, ESS 4,000) and eleven of twelve stay inside the basin, but the estimate scales as 1/γ: it is an estimate of the localization prior, not of the local geometry. There is no plateau. It is reported as non-identifiable under the declared criteria, rather than tuned until it yields a number.

  • ×The Gettier test was not falsifiable as I wrote it.

    The luck subset has zero marginal entropy. See section 04.

08

Corrections after external review

An independent review of the first version of this report found two technical defects. Both were real and both affected central numbers.

  • 1The “5,357-byte” bound was not a valid description.

    The code verified that the standardizer could be folded, but when measuring size it simply deleted mean and scale from an artifact whose weights had been quantized in standardized space. Without the standardizer, that file could not be applied to raw activations. The correct procedure — fold, then compress, then evaluate on raw inputs — gives 13,210 bytes and 397×, not 979×. The same held for the compressed MLP, which ended at 177,506. The measuring function now refuses any artifact whose standardizer is not the identity, so the invalid measurement cannot be repeated.

  • 2The bootstrap merged copies of the same group.

    See section 06. Fixed at the shared root; the 400 draws were repeated.

  • 3The corrected bootstrap used a single probe seed.

    Second review. Each draw trained with seed 2026 although the protocol declares five. Multi-seed support with per-draw averaging before ranking was added; the protocol-conforming run (400 × 5) reproduces the control's levels within 0.005 with narrower intervals, and is the one reported.

  • 4Three interpretations softened.

    “Conjunction” became “compatible with a conjunctive composition”; “belief contributes nothing” became “no detectable improvement, necessity not identifiable” because of near-total collinearity; and the design factor gettier was separated from the model's judgement lucky_guessed.

10

What the paper can claim

  • 1A bounded, measured bound.

    The rule by which this model decides whether someone knows something fits in a serialized payload of 13,210 bytes (median of five seeds; range 8,089–13,210) applicable to its raw activations under the declared format, with 0.980 macro-F1 on untouched data: 397 times less than the float32 MLP, of which 256× from the switch to a linear probe and 1.55× from quantization. It answers both parts of the reviewer's section 5: the naive bound was loose by two and a half orders of magnitude, and the bounded object does yield an informative number.

  • 2Compact and essentially linear decoding.

    A 5,121-parameter linear probe reads KNOWS off the activations more cheaply than a 1.3-million-parameter MLP. The structure is there in nearly linear form.

  • 3A nonlinear summary of the JTB components preserves almost all of the codelength.

    Three of the model's own epistemic scores suffice, and combining them requires a nonlinearity. Compatible with a conjunctive reading; not a demonstration of it. On belief, the design cannot decide.

  • 4A caution about the section 3.1 move.

    Dimensionality reduction does not tighten the standalone bound once what is needed to apply it to raw activations is counted: even the uncompressed linear probe is smaller.

  • 5Consistent minima between layers 26 and 29, final layer generally worse.

    Five predicates, two probe kinds, a three-layer band.

  • 6Two ordering claims, and nothing more.

    Under paired resampling and five seeds: lucky guess is the most expensive rule to decode, and justification is cheaper than knowledge. Belief, truth, knowledge and justification are not ranked among themselves beyond that; not separating is not being equally complex.

  • 7And explicit limits.

    One model, one scenario design, two failed replications, one non-identifiable measure, and three failed predictions plus one partial inside the bootstrap itself.
